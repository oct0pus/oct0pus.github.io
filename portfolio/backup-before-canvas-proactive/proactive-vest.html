The companion app for a self-inflating vest that eases sensory overload

Overview

ProActive is a reusable, self-inflating vest for people with autism and sensory sensitivities. I designed the app that pairs with it, end to end: I turned the research team's findings into features, then designed the flows, the screens and the colour system, so wearers and caregivers can see what the vest senses, control it, and learn from each episode.

How the vest works

Sensory overload happens in everyday places.

Sound, light, crowds and touch can all trigger it.

  1. 01Sensors in the vest detect rising stress.
  2. 02The vest inflates to apply firm, even pressure.
  3. 03It deflates once the wearer is calm.

I. Problem and Goals p. 1

I turned the startup's research into the app's brief. Because the app and the vest are being built at the same time, I keep reiterating the design as the hardware changes and the research team learns more. With this user base, I made accessibility my first priority throughout.

Goals

  1. 01A visible, accessible emergency help screen
  2. 02A calming colour scheme that still passes contrast checks
  3. 03Many functions, with navigation that stays clear

Two buttons, nothing else

I cut this screen down to two actions. In the moment, the wearer only needs to stop the vest or start calming down; everything else waits until the episode is over.

II. Research p. 2

The research team ran user interviews and A/B tests. My role was to turn what they found into design decisions.

What they found → what I designed

  1. 01Wearers and caregivers couldn't tell what caused their stress → Trigger analysis that names the likely cause of each episode
  2. 02Wearers needed guidance when stress peaked → Guided meditation offered at the moment stress rises
  3. 03Wearers wanted privacy from caregivers, as some episodes were personal → Caregiver notes kept alongside a private journal

From data to next steps

I made every insight end in something the wearer can do, such as adjusting the vest's sensitivity, with a quick “Helpful?” check.

III. The App p. 3

I designed the app around three jobs: show what the vest is sensing, give the wearer control, and help wearers and caregivers learn from past episodes.

Private by default

I made journal entries private by default. They're marked “Only you can see this”, and stay out of shared reports unless the wearer turns them on.

IV. Iteration Example p. 4

In my first version of the data log, people had to open each section in turn to understand one episode. I realised the order of information was the problem, and rebuilt it as a shorter, cleaner flow.

V. Reflection p. 5

This was my first time working with a startup on an ongoing basis. As a freelancer I usually run the research myself; here I took input from the research team. The team's capabilities kept changing, so I recreated the app many times to match new goals. Throughout this process I learned more about designing accessible screens, especially following the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG).
